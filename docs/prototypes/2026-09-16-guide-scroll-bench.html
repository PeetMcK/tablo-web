Moving the guide without a horizontal wheel

The time scrollbar is in and deployed. What is still open is whether the guide also wants a nudge control, and where it would live — so the chevrons are back, rebuilt as rails inside the header strip rather than buttons floating over the listings.

Time scrollbar

shipped

The horizontal bar is back under the listings; the vertical one stays hidden. A plain wheel still only moves channels, but now there is something to drag, and the bar's thumb is the first thing the guide has ever shown about how much of a fortnight sits off to the right. Firefox gets a thin bar on both axes — scrollbar-width takes no axis argument.

Header rails

mockup

Chevrons as thin rails in the header strip, the full height of the date and hour rows. The left rail begins where the timeline begins — the frozen channel column is not its to cover — and the right one sits against the frame edge. They page by a screenful less an hour, and grey out at the ends of the guide. Nothing overlaps a programme, and the listings keep every pixel they have.

Two-speed strip

mockup

The same rails and the same drag, geared to the row you grabbed. The hour row moves the guide one-for-one, for nudging around an evening. The date band moves it four times as far, because a band is a day — a throw across it should cover days. The cost is honest: geared, the listings no longer track your pointer, so the date band slides faster than the hand holding it.

Two-speed strip, no rails

mockup

The gearing on its own: grab the hours to nudge, grab the date to cover days, and nothing is added to the guide at all. This one also keeps the scrollbar that shipped, because that is the pairing a release would actually make — a bar for the person who never thinks to drag a header, and the strip for the person who does. The whole case against it is that second person has to discover it unaided.

Grab the listings too

mockup

The two-speed strip, plus the listings themselves as a drag surface at one-to-one in both directions, with the same throw. A programme still opens on a click — what separates the two is four pixels of travel: move less than that and it is a click, more and it is a pan and the chip does not open. Click one to see the stand-in sheet; then drag from the middle of a chip and watch it stay shut.

Grab the listings, axis locked

mockup

The same drag, committed to one direction. Whichever way you had travelled furthest at the moment you crossed the four-pixel threshold is the axis you get, and it holds for the rest of the gesture — re-deciding mid-drag would swap the axis under your hand every time a stroke wandered. The throw is locked with it, so a sideways flick never drifts the channel rows. Drag diagonally and compare with the bench above.

Header rails, on approach

mockup

The same rails, invisible until the pointer is inside the guide. At rest the grid is exactly what ships today, which is the argument for it; the argument against is that a control nobody has seen is a control nobody knows about, and the scrollbar already covers the person who never hovers. Ruled out

Floating chevrons

Round buttons centred over the listings, the first cut of this idea. They covered programmes, moved with nothing, and the back one parked itself on top of the channel column.

god awful — your words

Wheel over the clock

Pointing at the header made a plain wheel move time. It works, and it is invisible: the axis you get depends on a pointer position nobody tracks, and no other application behaves this way.

too non-standard to surface

Still open: arrow keys

The scroller takes no focus, so ← → and Page Up / Down do nothing. One tabindex fixes it, it conflicts with none of the above, and it is the only option here that answers someone who never touches a mouse.

not built yet

Every grid above is live: drag, hover and click them. Listings are your own guide mirror read at build time — 10 channels, 327 airings, 30 hours from the top of the hour. Hour columns are narrowed from the app's 400px so more than one fits here; the date band, frozen channel column and red now line are the shipped layout.
